References

Everything on this site is written in our own words from the six sources below. The small numbers in the text, such as [3], link to the matching entry on this page.

Reference list (APA 7th edition)

Entries are in alphabetical order. Web pages were last checked on November 11, 2026.

  1. Bennett, L. (2011, March 3). 10 tips for a successful business dinner. SavvyMiss.

    Course reading for SIE 415/515. We could not find a working link to the original web page, so none is given. Used in Plan Ahead and At the Table.

  2. Brooklyn College. (n.d.). How can I show proper business dinner etiquette? BC Knowledge for Students. Retrieved November 11, 2026, from https://students.brooklyn.edu/knowledge-base/how-can-i-show-proper-business-dinner-etiquette/

    Used for the place setting, arriving on time and ordering.

  3. Carreño, M. A. (1875). Compendio del manual de urbanidad y buenas maneras (26th ed.). Benito Gil. https://archive.org/details/compendiodelmanu00carr (Original work published 1853)

    Digitized by the Library of Congress. We cite Chapter V, Article III, “De la mesa,” pp. 100–114. Used in Carreño and in the notes of the place setting.

  4. Dizik, A. (2011, January 27). How to charm and do business over dinner. The Wall Street Journal.

    Course reading for SIE 415/515, based on an interview with Nina Zagat. We could not verify a public link to the article, so none is given. Used in Plan Ahead and At the Table.

  5. Memoria Chilena. (n.d.). Manuel Antonio Carreño (1812-1874). Biblioteca Nacional de Chile. Retrieved November 11, 2026, from https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-95831.html

    Used for the life of Carreño and the history of his manual.

  6. University of Nebraska–Lincoln, College of Business. (n.d.). Dining etiquette career resources. Business Career Center. Retrieved November 11, 2026, from https://business.unl.edu/business-career-center/dining-etiquette-career-resources/

    Used for the place setting, utensil positions, the napkin, phones and alcohol.
