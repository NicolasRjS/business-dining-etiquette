Quiz

Ten questions about planning the meal, reading the place setting, behaving at the table and the rules of Carreño. Every answer is explained somewhere on this site.

The 10 questions

  1. You are hosting a business dinner. Which restaurant is the safest choice?

    Choose a place where you already know the menu and the service, where you can talk, and that is convenient for your guest. See Choose the mood and the place.

  2. Your guest orders first and picks a mid-priced dish. What should you order?

    Letting the guest order first and then matching the price range keeps everyone comfortable. See The price-matching rule.

  3. There is a small plate on each side of you. Which bread plate is yours?

    Solids on the left, liquids on the right: your bread plate is at the upper left and your glasses at the upper right. See the place setting.

  4. You find two forks to the left of your plate. Which one do you use for the first course?

    Utensils are used from the outside in, so you start with the outer fork. The piece above the plate is for dessert. See the place setting.

  5. You need to leave the table for a moment during the meal. Where does your napkin go?

    A napkin on the chair shows that you are coming back. It returns to the table only when the meal is over. See Napkin.

  6. What should you do with your phone during a business meal?

    The phone stays off the table and should never ring. See Phone.

  7. When is the best moment to bring up your main business points?

    Start with small talk and get to know the other person, but do not wait until dessert for the serious points. See Conversation and when to talk business.

  8. According to Carreño, what is the only purpose of the napkin?

    Carreño says the napkin has no other purpose than wiping the lips. See What you will find on the table.

  9. A server makes a serious mistake with your order. What does Carreño tell you to do?

    He writes that we must never speak to servers in a commanding and harsh tone, however serious the mistake. See How to treat servers.

  10. Which of these rules from Carreño is considered dated today?

    This rule reflects the gender roles of the nineteenth century. Today every guest receives the same attention. See Carreño vs. today.
