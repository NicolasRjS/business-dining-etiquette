Before the meal

Plan Ahead

Most of a successful business meal is decided before anyone sits down. If you are the host, four choices matter: the place, what you know about your guest, the reservation, and how you will order.

1. Choose the mood and the place

The restaurant and the people you invite set the tone of the whole meal, so choose them on purpose.[1] A good business restaurant is one where you can hear each other and nobody has to think about the setting.

  • Go somewhere you already know. You should be familiar with the menu, the quality and the service. A business meal is not the time to experiment.[1]
  • Make it easy for your guest. Pick a location that is convenient and familiar for them, not for you.[1]
  • Look for quiet. Tables set far apart, or a restaurant with several small rooms, make conversation easier.[4]
  • Skip theme restaurants. Noise and gimmicks compete with the conversation.[1]
  • Stay in the middle on price. A mid-range restaurant keeps everyone comfortable when it is time to order.[1]
Seating a group

Round tables make everyone feel included. Avoid sitting at the head of a very long table. As host, seat your guest of honor on your right. If there are only two of you, ask your companion which seat they prefer.[4]

2. Know your guest

Ask before you book. Find out what your guest likes to eat and which part of town works for them.[4] Learn about tastes and allergies in advance, so the menu is never a problem at the table.[1]

If you cannot find out, choose a neutral restaurant with a varied menu. Almost everyone can find something there.[1]

3. Reserve, and be on time

Always make a reservation. Standing in line with a guest suggests that you did not organize the meeting well.[1] Many restaurants accept bookings online through services such as OpenTable (opens in a new tab).

Leave with enough time to cover traffic and other delays. If you arrive much too early, wait in a nearby coffee shop instead of at the table.[2]

4. The price-matching rule

Let your guest order first. Then order a dish that costs about the same, so that nobody feels awkward about what they chose.[1]

The rule works in both directions. If you are the guest, avoid the most expensive items on the menu.[2] The host normally pays the bill, so thank them for the meal and for the invitation.[2]

5. Then relax

Planning is what lets you stop worrying. Once the place, the guest and the table are taken care of, the best advice is the simplest: be yourself.[1]

Host checklist

  • I have eaten at this restaurant before.
  • It is quiet and easy for my guest to reach.
  • I asked about food preferences and allergies.
  • The table is reserved.
  • I know my route and will arrive on time.
  • My guest orders first; I match the price range.
